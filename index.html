Judging Outputs

Today you become an evaluator of AI systems. Instead of trusting an answer because it sounds sure, you decide whether it is correct, incorrect or unclear, and you prove your decision with evidence.

What you will learn

Words to know

criteria
The agreed things you judge an output against, such as accuracy, clarity and reliability.
incorrect
The output is factually false when you check it against a trusted source.
unclear
The output is so vague or ambiguous that you cannot tell whether it is right.
evidence
The exact word, fact or step you point to that proves your judgement.
reliability
Whether the same system would give a dependable result again, not a one-off fluke.

Why it matters

Across the UAE, people rely on AI to draft reports, translate and answer questions in seconds. But a wrong figure in a government form or an unclear medical instruction matters. Judging whether an output is correct, clear and reliable before you use it keeps you, not the tool, in charge of the result.

Part 1 of 3: Two kinds of fault

An output is incorrect when it states something false that you can check against a trusted source. It is unclear when you cannot tell what it means or whether it is right.

Incorrect
The facts are wrong. Fix: correct the facts using a trusted source.
Unclear
There is not enough detail. Fix: add detail and context, make the prompt specific.

Naming which fault you found is the first move of a good evaluator.

An AI system is made of components that interact. When an output is incorrect or unclear, the fault usually sits in one of them, not in some mysterious brain.

Part 3 of 3: Clear can still be wrong

A model generates text by predicting likely words, not by knowing what is true. So a fluent, confident answer can still be wrong or vague. An output can be perfectly clear and still be incorrect, which is exactly why you verify the fact.

Skilled evaluators cite specific evidence for every judgement and trace the fault back to its component.

Away from the screen: The Evaluation Panel

Act as an evaluation panel. Judge a set of AI outputs for one task against agreed criteria, decide whether each is correct, incorrect or unclear, and trace every fault to its component.

You need: output card set, criteria reference card (accuracy, clarity, reliability), trusted-source sheet, evidence sticky notes, component tags (data / prompt / tool).

  1. As a class, agree the criteria: accuracy, clarity and reliability.
  2. Read the task the outputs were meant to complete.
  3. Decide correct, incorrect or unclear for each output, against every criterion.
  4. Write one piece of evidence for every verdict: the exact word, fact or step.
  5. Verify one output against a trusted source to test whether a confident answer is accurate.
  6. Name the component behind each fault and share your strongest judgement.

No display? Use the printed cards: sort into correct, incorrect and unclear piles, each with an evidence note and a component tag.

English link

Evaluate a piece of writing against success criteria, citing the exact word or fact as evidence, and separate a factual error from merely unclear expression.

Lesson summary

  • Incorrect means false against a trusted source; unclear means you cannot tell.
  • Every judgement points to specific evidence, not a general impression.
  • A fault usually sits in a component (data, prompt or tool), not a mysterious brain.
  • Fixing the right component improves the whole system, not just one re-run.
